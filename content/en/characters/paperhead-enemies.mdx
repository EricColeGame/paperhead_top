## Detailed Breakdown of Common PAPERHEAD Enemies

### Paper Drones

Paper Drones are the most frequently encountered enemies in PAPERHEAD, appearing in nearly every zone from the earliest areas to late-game corridors. They're lightweight, fast, and individually weak — but they almost never appear alone.

**Key traits:**
- Low health pool, dies in one or two hits
- Moves in unpredictable zigzag patterns
- Often spawns in groups of five or more
- Can be staggered easily with any attack

The biggest danger with Paper Drones isn't any single one of them — it's the chaos they create in groups. Community reports suggest that many players underestimate early drone swarms and burn through resources trying to fight them head-on. A better approach is to use crowd-control abilities or environmental hazards to thin the herd before engaging directly.

### Cardboard Brutes

Cardboard Brutes are the tanks of the PAPERHEAD enemy lineup. Their thick, layered cardboard construction makes them resistant to standard attacks, and their slow movement gives players a false sense of security.

| Stat | Value |
|---|---|
| Health | High |
| Damage per hit | Very High |
| Movement Speed | Slow |
| Weak Point | Spine crease (back) |
| Resistance | Frontal attacks |

The key to defeating a Cardboard Brute is patience. Circling behind them to target the spine crease — a visual seam running down their back — dramatically increases your damage output. Player experience indicates that fighting them head-on is a losing strategy unless you're significantly overleveled or have armor-piercing abilities equipped.

### Origami Stalkers

Origami Stalkers are arguably the most unsettling enemies in the game. Their folded, angular forms allow them to flatten against walls and ceilings, making them nearly invisible until they strike. They tend to appear in darker zones and narrow corridors, where their ambush tactics are most effective.

**Combat tips for Origami Stalkers:**
- Listen for a faint rustling sound — this is your only audio cue before an ambush
- Use light-based abilities or torches to reveal hidden Stalkers
- They have moderate health but are vulnerable to fire damage
- Once revealed, they become aggressive but lose their stealth bonus

## Enemy Weaknesses and Resistance Chart

One of PAPERHEAD's most rewarding systems is its elemental interaction model. Every enemy has specific weaknesses and resistances tied to their material composition. Paper-based enemies, for example, are universally weak to fire and water damage — fire scorches them quickly, while water causes them to warp and lose structural integrity.

| Enemy Type | Weak To | Resistant To | Immune To |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper Drones | Fire, Water | Blunt | Fold damage |
| Cardboard Brutes | Piercing, Fire | Blunt, Slash | None |
| Origami Stalkers | Fire, Light | Slash | Darkness effects |
| Folded Sentinels | Water, Blunt | Piercing | Fold damage |
| Torn Wretches | Fire | None | None |
| Laminate Elites | Piercing | Fire, Water, Blunt | Fold damage |

Laminate Elites are the most resistant enemy class in the game, which is why players often find them so frustrating. Their laminated coating deflects fire and water damage almost entirely, making piercing attacks the go-to strategy. Community reports from experienced players emphasize building specifically for piercing damage output before attempting zones where Laminate Elites appear frequently.

## Advanced Strategies for Handling Tough PAPERHEAD Enemies

Once you've mastered the basics of each enemy type, the real skill ceiling in PAPERHEAD opens up. Here are advanced tactics that experienced players swear by:

### Staggering and Interrupt Windows

Most PAPERHEAD enemies have a brief stagger window during their wind-up animations. Learning to interrupt heavy attacks — especially from Cardboard Brutes and Laminate Elites — can completely neutralize their damage output. Player experience suggests that timing an interrupt correctly on a Cardboard Brute's slam attack will leave it open for a full three-second combo window.

### Environmental Exploitation

PAPERHEAD's environments are filled with interactive elements that can be weaponized against enemies. Water pools will weaken paper-based enemies on contact, fire sources can be triggered to create burning zones, and narrow corridors can be used to funnel swarming enemies into manageable choke points.

### Prioritization in Mixed Groups

When facing groups that include multiple enemy types, always prioritize in this order:

1. Origami Stalkers (eliminate before they can ambush)
2. Laminate Elites (highest damage threat)
3. Cardboard Brutes (high health, slow — manageable last)
4. Folded Sentinels (disable ranged threats early)
5. Paper Drones and Torn Wretches (clean up swarms last with AoE)

For more in-depth combat mechanics and official enemy data, check out [the PAPERHEAD community wiki and official game resources](https://store.steampowered.com) where developers and players collaborate to document every encounter in detail.

## FAQ: PAPERHEAD Enemies

**Q: What are the hardest PAPERHEAD enemies in the game?**
A: Laminate Elites are widely considered the most difficult PAPERHEAD enemies due to their high resistances, multi-phase attack combos, and the precision required to defeat them. Origami Stalkers are a close second because of their unpredictable ambush mechanics.

**Q: Do PAPERHEAD enemies respawn after I clear a zone?**
A: Based on player experience, most standard enemies do respawn when you leave and re-enter a zone, though elite-tier enemies like Laminate Elites may have longer respawn timers or remain cleared until a new game cycle begins.

**Q: What's the best way to deal with Paper Drone swarms?**
A: Area-of-effect abilities are your best friend against Paper Drone swarms. Using environmental fire sources or water hazards can also help thin groups quickly without burning through your ability cooldowns.

**Q: Are there any secret or hidden PAPERHEAD enemies not listed in the bestiary?**
A: Community reports suggest there are at least a few hidden enemy variants tucked into optional areas and secret rooms. These tend to be stronger versions of existing enemy types with unique drop tables, so exploring thoroughly is always worthwhile.
